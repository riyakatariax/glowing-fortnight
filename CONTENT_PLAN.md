# Portfolio website: content plan

A working plan for Riya Kataria's portfolio site, based on the four versions of the CV
(Comms, Entertainment/Music, Business, Politics) and Riya's own description of what she does.

## Positioning

**Riya reads culture and explains the why behind people's behaviour, then turns that into
decisions about what to make, say or sell.**

This is one skill, used in four fields. It's what cultural strategists, brand strategists /
strategic planners, consumer insights teams and trend forecasters do, so the site should
present it as a skill, not as a mix of unrelated interests.

The method, in three steps:

1. **Signal:** what the numbers show. *Gen Z is driving a rise in cinema attendance.*
2. **Why:** the cultural or behavioural cause. *Gen Z is increasingly isolated, and today's
   counterculture becomes tomorrow's culture. A cinema works almost like a third space: close
   to other people with no pressure to talk to them. Low stakes, still together.*
3. **So what:** what someone should do about it. *For a studio: event screenings and fan
   rituals. For a brand: sponsor low-pressure shared experiences. For a campaign: watch
   parties instead of rallies.*

Step 3 is what makes the thinking hireable, so every piece on the site should end with it.

Draft homepage lines (pick one, or rewrite):

- "Numbers tell you what people are doing. I figure out why, and what to do next."
- "I study why culture moves, and I use it in media, politics and business."
- "Cultural strategist. I read the room, at scale."

Sub-line: Oxford PPE · LSE MSc Media & Communications (Distinction) · LBS MiM (Merit Scholar).
U.S. citizen, based in [city].

## Culture Notes

The centrepiece. They show how you think, which a CV can't. Each note is 400 to 800 words:
**signal → why → so what**, with the "so what" addressed to a specific industry. One graph or
image is enough. Start with 3 notes, then add one a month.

Ideas, one for each field:

1. **Media / entertainment: "Why Gen Z went back to the movies."** The cinema-as-third-space
   argument. So what: studios and cinemas should design for the shared experience, not only
   the film.
2. **Politics:** what a trend in political culture (e.g. how young voters get their news, or
   the rise of creator-led campaigning) says about how policy should be communicated.
   Your Khanna press office experience gives you standing here.
3. **Music:** something you've seen first-hand through CRESCENDO, such as why certain artists'
   fanbases feel like communities. So what: how a label or brand should engage those fans.
4. **Business:** a product or brand that is winning or losing because of a cultural shift.
   So what: where it should launch its next product or service.

## Work (case studies)

Tell each project through your method: **what I noticed → what I did → what happened**.
No graphics needed. Numbers, a short story and the occasional screenshot or photo are enough.

1. **Portland Queer Arts Project: 500% follower growth.** What did you understand about the
   audience that the organisation didn't? Then the strategy (multi-channel, Google/Meta ads,
   A/B testing) and results (20+ performers engaged, new sponsors). Add screenshots of the
   account if they're still live.
2. **Rep. Ro Khanna's office: press and rapid response.** 15+ press communications, letters
   to President Biden on inflation and foreign policy, rapid-response social for 1.2M+
   followers. Link the public releases; add your Hill photos.
3. **CRESCENDO Magazine: U.S. coordinator.** Spotting emerging artists early and working with
   Chappell Roan, Rebecca Black, Remi Wolf, Lola Young. Concert photos, pull quotes, links.
4. **Oxford Taylor Swift Society: building a fandom from zero.** A good example of reading
   fandom as culture. Fastest-growing society, 100K+ organic impressions, 5,000+ attendees,
   coverage in The Times.
5. **Riya Kataria Photography: founding a business.** Clients in 10+ countries, self-built
   website, SEO, branding. Presented as entrepreneurship, not a hobby.
6. **LBS Out in Business / EurOut.** Social strategy for Europe's largest LGBTQ+ student
   conference across 30+ universities. Written case study only.
7. *(Optional, entertainment roles)* **OxRecords: student record label.** A&R, artist branding
   for 10+ artists, industry workshops.

## Notes and cautions

- **Fan edits:** they use copyrighted TV footage and music, so keep them on YouTube/TikTok and
  embed from there instead of hosting the files. Present them as proof you know what makes
  fans react. Leave them off the version you send to political offices.
- **Congress photos:** only post material that was already public or that you're sure is fine
  to share. When in doubt, ask the office.
- **Wedding photos:** get permission from the couples if you don't have it already.
- **CV alignment:** consider adding a one-line profile at the top of each CV that uses the
  same positioning as the site, and put the site URL at the top of every CV.
